    THE CENTRAL LIMIT THEOREM

    Averages become approximately normally distributed—even when individual values are not.

    Suppose we repeatedly draw independent observations from the same population with a finite mean and variance. As the sample size n grows, the distribution of the sample mean becomes increasingly well approximated by a normal distribution.

    The population stays right-skewed. It is the distribution of sample means that changes shape.

    Where are the means centered?
    E(X̄) = μ = 8

    The average of many sample means is close to the population mean.

    How variable are the means?
    SD(X̄) = σ / √n

    Here, the standard deviation of sample means is 8 / √n minutes.

    For n = 1, the theoretical SD of the sample mean is 8.00 minutes.

    The two formulas above are exact for independent observations with the stated population mean and SD. The normal shape is an approximation that generally improves as n grows.
